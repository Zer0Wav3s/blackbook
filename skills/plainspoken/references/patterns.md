# Editing patterns

Adapted from the original Plainspoken writing workflow. Use the full scan on ordinary authored prose and rewrite rhetorical hits. A listed word inside accurate domain terminology, a quotation, or required syntax is not itself a rhetorical hit. Preserve citations and the requested format. This table is a style preference, not proof of machine authorship.

Also cut: "passionate about", "resonates with me", "aligns with my", "I'm excited", "at its core", "the real question is", "fundamentally,", "in order to", "due to the fact that", "let's dive in", "without further ado", "I hope this helps", "let me know if", tidy rhetorical closers ("which is the shape of this job"), balanced "X rather than Y" cappers, and any sentence about your own honesty.

## Closers (the hits that survive a first pass)

The last sentence is where drafts relapse. These are review cues as a closing line, and weak anywhere:

- A verdict about the argument: "and that gap is the whole argument", "which is the shape of this job", "and that's the job".
- A balanced preference: "I'd rather X than Y", "X rather than Y", "not X but Y".
- A pointer instead of a statement: "the part where …", "the part that …", "what matters is …".
- A tidy generalisation after a specific: "…, and that's true of every team."

Close on the specific instead. If the last sentence could end any essay, it isn't yours.
